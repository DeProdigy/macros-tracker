# 11 — Feature: Post-Day Analysis (nightly, premium)

## What it is

At the end of each day, the app produces a recap of how the day went and how to do better tomorrow. Three layers:

1. **Score & totals** — how the day landed against targets
2. **Patterns** — trends across recent days, not just today
3. **Coaching** — specific swaps ("you ate X, next time try Y") and encouragement

Layers 1 and 2 are deterministic and free. Layer 3 is the AI layer, generated by a nightly job, and is the intended **premium feature** later.

## The economic design — read this first

The rest of the app runs AI synchronously behind explicit user action, for cost control (see plan doc 08). This feature deliberately does the opposite: it generates AI content automatically, nightly, per user. That only makes sense because:

* The **expensive layer is gated.** Free users get the deterministic recap (scoring, patterns) computed for nothing. Only the AI coaching layer runs the nightly job
* It's designed as a **premium feature from day one.** The nightly job checks a per-user flag before spending. In v1 everyone's flag may be the same, but the check exists so turning on billing later is a flip, not a migration

This is the difference between a cost leak and a line item you eventually charge for.

## Layer 1 — Score & totals (deterministic, free, immediate)

Plain arithmetic over the day's entries. No AI, no job — computed on read.

* Totals vs targets for calories, protein, fiber
* A simple day score: hit protein floor? stayed near calorie target? hit fiber?
* Reads against the day's own `TargetVersion`, so a past day scores against the targets that were real then

Available the moment the user opens the app. This is the recap free users see.

## Layer 2 — Patterns (deterministic, free)

Queries over recent `DailyLog` history, not AI guesses. Genuinely better computed than prompted — "you miss fiber 4 days out of 7" is a fact, not an inference.

* Recurring misses ("fiber is your most-missed target this week")
* Streaky behaviors ("protein is consistently on-point")
* Simple week-over-week movement

These are aggregation queries. Cheap, exact, and they don't hallucinate.

## Layer 3 — Coaching (AI, nightly job, premium)

The layer that costs money and justifies the job.

* **Swaps** — specific, grounded in what was actually eaten: "the 900-cal lunch is where the day tipped; a similar meal at ~500 would have kept you on target"
* **Encouragement first, tips second** — per the product decision. Lead with what went right, then the actionable nudge
* Uses the day's entries, the deterministic scoring, and pattern context as prompt input

Strict JSON output, validated. Same discipline as every other AI feature here.

## Architecture — this is where the queue enters the stack

The project has run AI synchronously until now, and plan doc 01 explicitly parks Celery until there's evidence it's needed. **This feature is that evidence.** A scheduled, per-user, nightly job cannot live in a request.

* **Celery + Redis** (or a lighter scheduler — evaluate at implementation) enters here
* Railway gains a **worker process** alongside the web process, and a scheduler/beat process for the nightly trigger. The deployment doc needs updating to reflect a multi-process deploy
* The job is idempotent — re-running for the same user-day produces one result, not duplicates
* Failures are retried with backoff and never block anything user-facing

### Per-user timezone scheduling — the subtle part

"Night" is per-user local time, not a single server-wide moment. You do **not** run one 2am job.

Instead: a periodic job (say hourly) finds users for whom it's now past end-of-day locally and whose previous day hasn't been analyzed yet, and processes that completed day.

"Which day is done" is a clean query thanks to `DailyLog`-as-entity — the payoff of that model showing up again. A day is final when the user's local clock has passed midnight *and* a small grace buffer, to absorb late entries and edits.

## When is a day "final"?

Analysis needs a settled day. Interactions to handle:

* **Late entries** — a 11:50pm snack logged at 12:10am (via "log to previous day") must be included. The grace buffer covers this
* **Edits after analysis** — if a user edits a past day, its analysis is stale. Either regenerate on next job pass or mark it stale. Simplest v1: analyze once, after the grace buffer; accept that rare post-hoc edits won't retrigger coaching
* **E10 / HealthKit** — unrelated to finality, but deletes that ripple to Health should also invalidate that day's recap

## Premium gating — build the seam now

No billing in v1. But the seam must exist:

* A nullable `tier` / `is_premium` field on the user (or a small `Subscription` model if you want room to grow)
* The nightly job checks it before generating Layer 3
* Layers 1 and 2 ignore it — always free

Backfilling a permission concept across a live user base is painful; adding a flag now that everyone shares is trivial. Do the cheap thing early.

## Delivery to the user

* Deterministic recap: visible immediately on opening the app for the just-completed day
* AI coaching (when premium/enabled): ready by morning, since the job ran overnight. A gentle "your recap is ready" is optional and, if added, is a push notification — its own small piece of setup

## What you'd be learning

* **Background job processing** — Celery, Redis, workers, scheduled/periodic tasks. A major backend skill this project has deliberately deferred until now
* Multi-process deployment on Railway (web + worker + scheduler)
* Idempotency and retry design for jobs that cost money
* Per-timezone scheduling, a deceptively fiddly real-world problem
* Feature-gating architecture ahead of billing

## Sequencing

After E10. This is the most infrastructurally ambitious feature in the project — it introduces the queue, a worker deploy, and the premium seam — so it belongs well after the core loop is real and used.

## Deferred within this feature

* Actual billing / payment (RevenueCat or StoreKit) — a separate epic when premium goes live
* Push notification for "recap ready" — additive
* Regenerating analysis after late edits — accept staleness in v1
